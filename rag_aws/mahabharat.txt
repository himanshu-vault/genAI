Here is the **Mahabharata story in plain text format**:

---

The Mahabharata is one of the greatest ancient Indian epics, written by the sage Vyasa. It tells the story of a great conflict between two groups of cousins—the Pandavas and the Kauravas—and explores themes like duty (dharma), righteousness, and the consequences of actions.

The story begins in the kingdom of Hastinapur, where two families grow up together. The Pandavas are five brothers—Yudhishthira, Bhima, Arjuna, Nakula, and Sahadeva—known for their virtues and strength. The Kauravas are one hundred brothers led by Duryodhana, who is jealous of the Pandavas.

As the princes grow older, Duryodhana’s jealousy increases. He plots against the Pandavas multiple times. Eventually, he invites them to a game of dice. Yudhishthira, the eldest Pandava, accepts the invitation. Due to deceit and manipulation by Shakuni, the Pandavas lose everything—their kingdom, their wealth, and even their freedom. In a shocking moment, Draupadi, the wife of the Pandavas, is humiliated in the royal court. This becomes a major turning point in the story.

After losing the game, the Pandavas are sent into exile for 13 years, including one year in hiding. During this time, they face many hardships but also prepare themselves for the future.

When their exile ends, they ask for their kingdom back, but Duryodhana refuses to give even a small piece of land. This leads to the great war of Kurukshetra.

Before the war begins, Arjuna feels confused and reluctant to fight against his own relatives and teachers. At this moment, Lord Krishna, who is his charioteer, gives him deep spiritual knowledge. This teaching is known as the Bhagavad Gita, where Krishna explains duty, righteousness, and the nature of life.

The war lasts for 18 days and involves massive destruction. Great warriors like Bhishma, Drona, and Karna fight bravely but are eventually defeated. The Pandavas, with Krishna’s guidance, emerge victorious. However, the victory comes at a huge cost, with immense loss of life on both sides.

After the war, Yudhishthira becomes the king and rules wisely. In the end, the Pandavas renounce their kingdom and journey towards the Himalayas in search of spiritual liberation.

The Mahabharata teaches that life is complex, and doing the right thing is not always easy. It shows that greed, ego, and jealousy lead to destruction, while truth and righteousness ultimately prevail.
